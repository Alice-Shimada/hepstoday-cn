In this work, we analyze the constraints induced by DAMIC-M and PandaX results on the reheating temperature in freeze-in models at stronger coupling, or when a non-thermal source (such as inflaton decay) comes into play. We identify viable scenarios in which the DM relic abundance is correctly reproduced while evading current experimental bounds on the electron-scattering cross section, $\overlineσ_\mathrm{e}$. In particular, we show that for reheating temperatures below the electroweak scale, Boltzmann suppressed production can be compensated by stronger couplings, bringing freeze-in scenarios within present experimental reach. Finally, we study a hybrid scenario in which a small branching ratio of inflaton decay seeds a nonzero initial dark-matter abundance. We show that such contributions can significantly modify freeze-in predictions across broad regions of parameter space, offering an additional pathway for probing extremely feeble interactions.
